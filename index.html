What I do

Four disciplines that separate AI that demos from AI that ships.

01 · AI EVALS & GOVERNANCE

Evals are the license to operate

I design the evaluation gates that get risk and compliance to yes: pass/fail bars agreed before build, prompt-injection resistance, bias checks, drift and override telemetry in production, and the audit trail that survives a regulator. In a regulated enterprise, the eval framework is the product decision.

Proof: the framework that moved fraud, claims and advisor AI past model risk review at a major bank
02 · LLM GATEWAY & COST ENGINEERING

One gateway, any model, a defensible unit cost

Provider-agnostic model routing, token budgets per interaction, context compaction, payload offloading, and run-cost forecasting the CFO can plan on. I treat the context window and the invoice as engineered resources, not surprises.

Proof: a working gateway across Anthropic, OpenAI, Bedrock, Vertex and Ollama, one env var to switch
03 · AIDLC · THE OPERATING DISCIPLINE

An SDLC built for AI, open-sourced

The Applied AI Delivery Lifecycle: structured discovery, quantified business cases, five executive decision gates, evaluation harnesses, and governance mapped to EU AI Act risk tiers and NIST AI RMF. It is how an organization ships AI the same way twice.

Proof: github.com/karthikbadri12/applied-ai-enterprise · PyPI: aidlc-studio
04 · AI PRODUCT MANAGEMENT

From boardroom question to production P&L

When executives ask where to apply AI, I return a prioritized portfolio, a quantified business case and value proposition for each, and the delivery plan through compliance into production, including the honest call on which problems do not need AI at all.

Proof: fraud networks, claims automation and advisor AI running in production at RBC Insurance
